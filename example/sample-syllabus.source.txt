I. COURSE DESCRIPTION AND PLACEMENT

Algebra 2 is a year-long course of study satisfying the third year of the
mathematics requirement for graduation from Ridgeview Unified School District
and meeting the University of California "c" subject requirement. The course
builds upon the standards addressed in Algebra 1 and Geometry, and prepares
students for subsequent enrollment in Precalculus, Statistics, or an
articulated dual-enrollment offering. Placement in this course is determined by
prior coursework and department placement guidance; enrollment changes after
the tenth instructional day require administrative approval per Board Policy
5121.

II. STANDARDS AND UNITS OF STUDY

Instruction is aligned to the California Common Core State Standards for
Mathematics, with particular emphasis on the following domains and clusters:
A-SSE.1-4, A-APR.1-6, A-REI.4-11, F-IF.4-9, F-BF.1-4, F-LE.1-5, N-CN.1-9, and
S-ID.6. Units of study, in sequence:

  Unit 1  Linear systems and matrices
  Unit 2  Quadratic functions and complex numbers
  Unit 3  Polynomial functions and the remainder theorem
  Unit 4  Rational and radical functions
  Unit 5  Exponential and logarithmic functions
  Unit 6  Sequences, series, and an introduction to probability
  Unit 7  Trigonometric functions and the unit circle

Standards-aligned pacing is subject to modification at the discretion of the
instructor and the mathematics department to accommodate site testing windows,
minimum days, and other calendar adjustments.

III. REQUIRED MATERIALS

Students are expected to arrive to each class session in possession of the
following: a bound or spiral notebook designated exclusively for this course; a
pencil (work submitted in ink may be returned for resubmission at the
instructor's discretion); the district-issued Chromebook, charged to a level
sufficient to complete a full instructional period; and a scientific
calculator. A graphing calculator is not required. A class set of TI-84 graphing
calculators is maintained in Room 214 and is available for in-class use only;
students requiring a calculator for home use may check one out through the
library subject to availability and the district technology use agreement.
Students experiencing financial hardship should contact the instructor or a
counselor; no student will be denied participation for inability to purchase
materials.

IV. EVALUATION AND DETERMINATION OF GRADES

Student achievement is measured through the following weighted categories:

  Unit Tests and Final Examinations .................. 55%
  Quizzes and Checkpoints ............................ 20%
  Homework and Classwork ............................. 15%
  Group Problem Sets and Presentations ............... 10%

Letter grades are assigned according to the following scale, and the instructor
does not round: A, 90 percent and above; B, 80 percent and above; C, 70 percent
and above; D, 60 percent and above; F, below 60 percent.

Grades of record are maintained in the ParentVue student information system.
The instructor updates the online gradebook weekly, ordinarily by Friday
afternoon. Progress reports are issued at the six-week and twelve-week marks of
each semester in accordance with Education Code section 49067 and Board Policy
5121. The semester grade is the grade of record for transcript purposes;
quarter grades are advisory only.

V. HOMEWORK AND LATE WORK

Homework is assigned on most instructional days and is generally expected to
require approximately thirty minutes. Homework is due at the beginning of the
class period on the date indicated in the online classroom.

Late homework and classwork will be accepted for a deduction of ten percent per
school day, to a maximum deduction of fifty percent, after which the assignment
may still be submitted for feedback but will be recorded at fifty percent
credit. No homework or classwork will be accepted after the last instructional
day of the grading period in which it was assigned.

Each student is issued four "extension tokens" per semester. One token, filed
through the online classroom on or before the original due date, moves that
assignment's deadline by two school days with no deduction. Tokens may not be
combined on a single assignment, do not carry over between semesters, and may
not be applied to tests, quizzes, or group presentations.

VI. ATTENDANCE AND MAKE-UP WORK

Regular attendance is required by Education Code section 48200. Following an
excused absence, a student has a number of school days equal to the number of
days absent to submit make-up work without penalty, per Board Policy 5113.
Students are responsible for obtaining missed material from the online
classroom before requesting instructor time.

A student who is absent on the day of an announced assessment is expected to sit
that assessment on the first day of return. Assessments are made up before
school, between 7:20 and 8:00 in Room 214, or during a scheduled academic
support period; assessments are not made up during instructional time.

For a planned absence of three or more school days, the parent or guardian
should request an independent study contract through the attendance office at
least five school days in advance, in accordance with Education Code section
51747.

Unexcused absences and truancies are addressed through the site attendance
policy and may result in referral to the Student Attendance Review Board.

VII. ASSESSMENT RETAKES

One retake is offered per unit test, excluding semester final examinations. To
be eligible, a student must have submitted all homework for the unit and must
complete a test correction form. Retakes are scheduled before school and must
be completed within ten school days of the return of the original assessment.
The recorded score is the higher of the two attempts, capped at 85
percent. Quizzes, checkpoints, and final examinations may not be retaken.

VIII. CLASSROOM EXPECTATIONS AND ELECTRONIC DEVICES

Students are expected to conduct themselves in a manner consistent with the
Ridgeview High School Student Code of Conduct and Board Policy 5131. Personal
electronic devices, including cellular telephones, smart watches, and wireless
earbuds, are to be powered off and stored in the classroom device caddy during
instructional time, consistent with the district's instructional-hours device
policy. A first occurrence results in a verbal redirection; a second occurrence
results in the device being held until the end of the period; a third
occurrence results in the device being delivered to the assistant principal's
office for parent pickup and a documented parent contact.

Food and beverages other than water are not permitted in Room 214 pursuant to
site facilities guidance.

IX. ACADEMIC INTEGRITY AND ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Academic dishonesty, including but not limited to copying another student's
work, permitting one's work to be copied, unauthorized communication during an
assessment, possession of unauthorized materials during an assessment, and the
submission of work generated in whole or substantial part by an artificial
intelligence system without instructor authorization, constitutes a violation
of Board Policy 5131.9. A substantiated violation results in a score of zero on
the assignment or assessment in question, a documented parent contact, and a
referral to administration; subsequent violations may result in further
disciplinary action up to and including removal from the course with a grade of
"F" at the discretion of administration.

Artificial intelligence tools may be used to generate practice problems or to
request an explanation of a concept already covered in class. Artificial
intelligence tools may not be used to produce work submitted for a grade. When
an AI tool has been used in preparation, the student is to note its use at the
top of the submitted work.
